Sanity checks: on a made-up sideways market (price swinging 95-105) the same simulator makes +14% in 13 months, and on real BTC with zero fees it still loses 40% while BTC rose 834%. So the losses are the grid's design meeting trending markets, not a bug.

## What it means
- **A grid sells every rally and buys every drop.** When price leaves the range downward, the grid ends up holding the full position all the way down; when price leaves it upward, it has sold everything early and sits in cash while the coin runs. Crypto trends far more than it ranges, so a grid keeps the losses and caps the gains.
- **More grids = worse:** smaller steps mean more trades, and each grid step must beat two fees (0.40% round trip with limit orders, 0.70% with market orders).
- **Maker vs taker matters** (−81% vs −90%), but no fee setting fixes the trend problem.
- A grid only fits a coin that truly stays in a range, which no tested range finder could predict (F12, N1). Not added. The RANGE pile stays empty.
